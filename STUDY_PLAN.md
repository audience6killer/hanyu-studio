# The six-month HSK5 plan

This is the reasoning behind the schedule the app generates. The app recalculates it from your real target date and daily minutes; this document is the map, not a second source of truth.

## Starting point

- You finished the HSK4 course, then stopped for a few years.
- You are returning at HSK5, not starting from zero.
- No speaking practice is required.
- Primary study time is at work: 30–45 focused minutes on weekdays, approximately five days a week.
- Target: sit the HSK5 exam in roughly six months.

## Two assumptions worth stating

1. **Six months is ambitious at 35 minutes a day.** The schedule targets it, but the app will tell you if the pace is slipping rather than quietly pretending. A 7–9 month horizon is more comfortable if reviews start to pile up.
2. **Recognition comes before production.** Reading and listening need recognition of ~1,300 new words; writing needs production of a smaller core. The app schedules reading, recall and listening separately so you can see the difference.

## Weekly rhythm

| Day | Session |
|---|---|
| Monday | Reviews → new words → grammar |
| Tuesday | Reviews → new words → reading |
| Wednesday | Reviews → new words → listening |
| Thursday | Reviews → new words → writing |
| Friday | Reviews → new words → next stage; checkpoint when the plan calls for one |
| Weekend | Rest, or a short free review if you missed a weekday |

Each session is built from what is actually due, capped at your daily minutes. When the review queue is heavy, new words are reduced automatically; when it is empty, the session can run ahead.

## Six-month map

| Weeks | Lessons | Focus | Checkpoint |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1–2 | 1–2 | Calibration, HSK4 refresh, routine | — |
| 3–5 | 3–6 | Steady vocabulary, first reading and dictation sets | End of week 5: lessons 1–6 |
| 6–9 | 7–12 | Idioms and narrative reading; longer dictation | End of week 9: lessons 1–12 |
| 10–13 | 13–18 | Formal connectives and argument | End of week 13: lessons 1–18 |
| 14–17 | 19–24 | Description, reflection, abstract topics | End of week 17: lessons 1–24 |
| 18–21 | 25–30 | Work/everyday essays; faster reading | End of week 21: lessons 1–30 |
| 22–25 | 31–36 | Final lessons; exam technique | End of week 25: full mock |
| 26 | — | Consolidation, weak spots, logistics | Rest and final review |

The checkpoint weeks are approximate: the app places them after the lesson block, and only opens one once you have finished the block's lessons. A checkpoint is 45 minutes: 7 listening items, 15 reading items and one typed composition. It is a practice instrument, not an official paper.

## What covers what

| HSK5 exam section | How the app prepares you |
|---|---|
| Listening (45 items) | Listening review cards (word → meaning), 180 dictation sentences, passage audio with comprehension questions, checkpoint listening section |
| Reading (45 items) | Recognition cards, 36 original passages with 108 questions, grammar-in-context drills, tap-to-define during study |
| Writing (sentence completion + short composition) | Grammar drills, typed recall cards, 36 composition tasks with target words and models, checkpoint composition |
| Vocabulary (1,300 new words) | 1,367 book words in lesson order + 390 official-list gap words, each with reading, recall and listening schedules |
| Grammar | 36 curated HSK5 patterns, each with explanation, examples, common mistake and two drills |

Speaking is deliberately absent. Nothing in the app scores pronunciation.

## How to spend a 35-minute session

- 8–12 minutes: due reviews. Do not skip these to get to "new" material.
- 8–12 minutes: 12–15 new words from the current lesson.
- 12–15 minutes: one lesson stage, or grammar + reading when both are short.
- 2 minutes: close the tab and note whether you finished. The app records the rest.

If you only have 15 minutes, use **Quick review**. If you have 10 minutes and no reviews, read one passage without the translation.

## Pace rules

- **On pace** means completed lessons are within one lesson of the schedule.
- **Slightly behind (1–2 lessons):** keep the same settings; the plan's buffer absorbs it.
- **Behind by 3+ lessons:** the app raises new-word targets only if the review queue is light; otherwise it asks you to lower new words or extend the date. Choose one. Do not do both silently.
- **Ahead:** add a lesson stage or take the next checkpoint early. Do not add 20 new words a day just because you can; review debt arrives three weeks later.

## Readiness signals

The **Progress → Exam readiness** number is a composite: half vocabulary coverage of the official list, 30% review accuracy, 20% lesson coverage. It is a rough instrument. The more useful signals are:

- you recognize the majority of words in a lesson passage on first read;
- dictation accuracy is above 70% without replaying more than twice;
- you can write 80 characters using four target words in about 15 minutes;
- your last two checkpoints are at or above 65%.

If those hold, the date is credible regardless of the composite.

## At-work logistics

- Headphones for listening; keep the volume low.
- Use the 35-minute session as a single block, not five scattered checks. The timer tracks active minutes only.
- Export a backup at the end of each week. Work computers get reimaged.
- If audio is not available, do reading and recall in the office and save dictation for a quiet moment. The app does not require audio to schedule reviews.

## Weekly checklist

- [ ] Five sessions logged
- [ ] Review queue below ~80 cards
- [ ] Current lesson's vocabulary introduced
- [ ] One stage beyond vocabulary completed
- [ ] Backup exported (Friday)
- [ ] Checkpoint taken if it was due
